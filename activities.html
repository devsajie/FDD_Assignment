TECHNICAL MATRIX

Hackathon Tracks & Competition Rules

APUHACKERX2026 focuses on grounded engineering challenges. Review the track guidelines below, choose your team's focus area, and familiarize yourself with the integrity code.

TRACK 01

AI & Localized Intelligence

DIFFICULTY: ADVANCED // JUDGING WEIGHT: 25%

Most frontier AI architectures assume high-bandwidth connectivity and English datasets. This track challenges teams to build resilient, quantized, or edge-based models that understand low-resource languages, local civic datasets, or agricultural pathology in Nepal.

Example Problem Statements:

  • Offline-capable mobile OCR and voice assistants for Nepali government paper forms.
  • Early blight crop disease detection using lightweight edge neural networks on budget Android phones.
  • Automated legal and academic document summarization trained on open Supreme Court of Nepal archives.
RECOMMENDED STACK: PyTorch, ONNX, Hugging Face Transformers, Flutter, FastAPI, Ollama.
TRACK 02

Climate & Urban Resilience

DIFFICULTY: INTERMEDIATE // JUDGING WEIGHT: 25%

The Kathmandu Valley and mountainous river basins face escalating micro-climate stresses, including particulate air pollution inversions and monsoon flash floods. Teams will build IoT sensor telemetry and predictive dispatch platforms.

Example Problem Statements:

  • Low-cost LoRaWAN particulate sensor nodes for neighborhood air quality heatmapping.
  • Real-time river level acoustic telemetry alerting riverside settlements before flash flood crests.
  • Municipal waste collection route optimization using fill-level ultrasonic bin telemetry.
HARDWARE TESTBED: ESP32, DHT22, PMS5003 air sensors, LoRa transceivers (available on loan).
TRACK 03

FinTech & Digital Inclusion

DIFFICULTY: ADVANCED // JUDGING WEIGHT: 20%

Digitizing commerce beyond metropolitan centers. Create offline transaction verification, cross-border educational remittance calculators, or privacy-preserving digital identity systems for cooperatives and small merchants.

Example Problem Statements:

  • Cryptographic zero-knowledge proofs for student identity verification without exposing personal identifiers.
  • Offline-first peer-to-peer reconciliation for rural grocery ledgers using Bluetooth Low Energy.
CODE OF CONDUCT

Rules & Regulations

Compliance with the following standards is mandatory for all registered participants. Violations result in immediate disqualification.

01 // Fresh Code Requirement

All code, models, and assets must be generated during the official 48-hour event window (Nov 30 17:00 to Dec 02 12:00). Open source libraries, public APIs, and pre-trained foundation models are permitted with clear attribution.

02 // Team Integrity & Size

Teams must strictly consist of 2 to 4 currently enrolled university students. Solo participation is not permitted. Team members may not be substituted after check-in.

03 // Open Source & Verification

Repositories must be hosted on public GitHub or GitLab instances with visible commit histories showing regular work throughout the 48 hours. The jury reviews git logs during final scoring.

04 // Campus Conduct & Inclusivity

We are dedicated to providing a harassment-free experience for everyone regardless of gender, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, or religion. Mutual respect is strictly enforced.
